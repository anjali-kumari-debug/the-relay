1 What I built and why

Pipeline: admission (Groq-mirroring token buckets, FIFO queue, 20 s patience) → analyse (local keyword routing) → retrieve (tool call search_papers, BM25 over page-tagged chunks) → evidence gate (weak → expand once, else refuse without a model call) → draft (Groq openai/gpt-oss-20b, streamed) → verify (local: every [paper p.N] must be a retrieved page, every multi-digit number must appear in a cited passage; fail → one revise, else refuse). The decision that shaped it most: tokens, not requests or latency, are the scarce resource. An answer costs ~3.3K tokens, so 8,000 TPM allows ~2.4 answers/min (RPM would allow 30) and the daily token limit ~60 answers. Hence one model call per answer, deterministic analysis and verification, and under pressure a cheaper retrieval setting (A) instead of turning people away. "Fast" = first token ≤ 1 s at p95 when not overloaded; nobody waits over 20 s.

Finding: every limit is a continuously refilling bucket, not a counter that resets. TPM refills at 133 tokens/s and TPD at 2.3 tokens/s, and each retry-after equals the deficit divided by the refill rate.

Stand-in (src/relay/standin_server.py): a Groq-compatible SSE server (same client, different base_url) enforcing the measured buckets with Groq-shaped 429s. Calibrated: first token lognormal (median 420 / p95 800 ms; real p50 404/519 ms) + 58 ms per 1K prompt tokens (Groq prompt_time); ~1 token/chunk; 1.17× slower per extra request in flight above 2 (12-concurrent probe). It replays the recorded real answer per question and setting, so its rows carry real grades. Synthetic faults, never seen on Groq: 1% 5xx, 3% slow successes. Where it differs most: Groq's queue_time is 60–70% of its first-token time and depends on other tenants; mine only on my own traffic. Real 429s took 2–3 s under concurrency; mine are instant. Unmeasured: I assume unused max_completion_tokens are refunded to the TPM bucket, and Table 2's overload capacity depends on it.

4 Where the time goes

Slowest real request (6e2f7c76314f, q11, setting C): 12,037 ms = 10,408 waiting + 1,628 working + 0 retrying, and the delay came after the first token (820 ms). The draft finished at 889 ms but failed verification: it cited [paper_id p.10], copying our prompt's placeholder, and gave BPIC 6.0 (the heuristic baseline's figure, not 4.8). The revise call needed a 3.5K-token reservation and waited 10.4 s in our own admission queue (queue_enter → queue_exit), then returned the same text and was refused. Every phase boundary is timestamped in events.jsonl; scripts/slow_request.py prints the split.

5 When a limit is hit

Per-minute: requests wait FIFO for the bucket to refill. Below 30% TPM headroom new requests get setting A (weaker: 64.7% vs 76.5% correct). Whoever would exceed 20 s in the queue is dropped (gave_up_in_queue), so in a burst the latest arrivals lose. A provider 429 pauses all admissions for its retry-after. Per-day: nothing is queued or retried. The first TPD 429, or our guard at 90% of TPD, marks the day exhausted and everyone after gets the fixed reply in ~2 ms. In the real run Groq's 429 took 103 ms and the next two were refused locally in 1–2 ms. Our guard is a fixed counter, so it fired early (~180K tokens) and blocked 4 evaluation rows (I re-ran one and dropped q18). I reset it deliberately for real_steady to observe Groq's own response; Groq accepted ~20K more tokens because its bucket refills.

How this library will mislead a naive retriever

  1. Different words for the same thing. "What practical latency does Moshi report?" Page 1 says "200ms in practice". BM25 missed it, so all three settings answered "not reported".
  2. Symbols. "Adam beta1, beta2, epsilon?" The paper writes β1/β2/ϵ, so the best passage scored 0.36. My first gate (0.50) refused it before asking the model; I lowered the gate to 0.30.
  3. One word, several meanings; one result, two numbers. "Transformer" appears in all three papers: on q15, one answer cited H-Net for a claim about the original Transformer. Attention v7 reports 41.8 BLEU in its abstract and Table 2 but 41.0 in §6.1. One real answer said "41.0 in Table 2" (outputs/archive/adhoc_real/q02_A_rerun).

6 Retries

Policy: at most one retry, only on 429, timeout or 5xx; wait for retry-after, else full-jitter backoff (0.5–8 s); never on a daily limit, after tokens have streamed, or past the person's patience. SDK retries are off, so there is one retry layer. Where retrying made things worse (stand-in, client admission opened 10×, same 84 arrivals): retries raised completions from 43 to 57, but provider 429s rose from 41 to 58, because retried requests arrive just as the bucket refills. Each 429 restarts our global cool-down, so 34 requests waited over 5 s in our own queue against 0 without retries. d4a9ed359c28 (q07) never retried, arrived to an empty queue and waited 10.3 s. Median first token went from 642 ms to 9,739 ms, and 15 requests that succeeded without retries failed with them. On real data, the revise in heading 4 doubled one request's tokens for the same answer.

7 What the person sees

At 3 pm, within ~2 ms: "I can't answer right now: today's usage allowance for the language-model service has been used up. Daily allowances reset once a day, so please try again after the reset. I have not guessed an answer." My own measurement shows this is wrong: the bucket refills continuously, so it should say "try again in about N minutes", with N from retry-after (36 min in my run). That is my next change.

8 What I'm unsure about, what I left out, what I learned

Least certain assumption: that Groq's free-tier queueing stays near 300 ms under launch-day load from other tenants. If it grows, the stand-in's overload latencies are optimistic and 20 s patience drops more people than Table 2b shows. Deliberately not built: an embedding retriever (stemming would fix "practice/practical" without another model) and an answer cache (it saves tokens but hides the behaviour this brief asks me to measure).
